# Round 0: the 30 rough lines (Strata Lifts dry run, 2026-11-02)

Three Fable generators, ten lines each: 1-10 the subject's own world, 11-20 other crafts that answer "when is the next one", 21-30 the best shipped products in and out of the field. Scores and the cut are in `log.md`.

1. Elevation Sheet — mechanism: the first screen is a side elevation of the bridge on a drawing sheet, the two bascules drawn open to tonight's angle with a dimension arrow labelling degrees, the next lift time sitting in the title block bottom-right where a drawing's revision date goes; memorable: a dimension line that reads "34° at 19:45"; palette: blueprint-white paper, graphite line, one red dimension ink; type: stencilled drawing-office capitals for the title block, a narrow engineer's hand for dimensions; sources: Barry and Jones elevation drawings (sheet, title block, dimension lines), bridge-master's lift log (the degree and time pairing), Thames tide tables (the small-figures column of later lifts), Ordnance Survey map legend (restrained red for the one live fact)
2. Engine Room Gauge — mechanism: a single brass pressure-gauge dial fills the screen, its needle sweeping from "now" to the next lift as a countdown, the vessel name engraved on the dial face below the pivot; memorable: the needle creeping while you watch; palette: cast-iron black, aged brass, engine-room green paint, one white dial; type: engraved Victorian gauge numerals, small engraved caption capitals; sources: 1894 hydraulic engine room gauges (dial, needle, engraved face), ship's engine-order telegraph (the sweep from one state to the next), Underground platform countdown boards (minutes-to-next as the hero number), Armstrong accumulator paint (the green)
3. PLA Notice Board — mechanism: the screen is a printed Port of London Authority notice pinned at the riverside, headed "Notice to Mariners" style, today's lifts as a ruled table with the next one hand-stamped in red, the bascule angle drawn as a small margin diagram; memorable: the red rubber stamp over the next row; palette: notice-board buff paper, black letterpress, red stamp ink, a weathered pin-board frame; type: condensed letterpress grotesque headings, tabular figures in the body; sources: Port of London Authority notices (the ruled table and official heading), date-stamp on a railway ticket (the red stamp), Admiralty tide-table layout (times in columns, flood and ebb marks), Lloyd's Register vessel entries (name, type, tonnage in one line)
4. River Signal Lights — mechanism: a black screen with the bridge's own lift signal lamps at full size, red-over-red meaning closed, the lamps changing as the lift approaches, the time and vessel name set small beneath like a light-list entry, bascule angle as a thin outline above the lamps; memorable: two glowing lamps on a dark night, not a glow effect but a real lamp drawing; palette: night black, signal red, signal green, a thin white outline; type: Admiralty light-list monospace for the facts, nothing else; sources: Tower Bridge lift signal lights (the lamps and their meaning), Admiralty List of Lights (the characteristic notation and flat type), railway semaphore signal boxes (the two-state clarity), lighthouse lens pattern (the ringed lamp drawing)
5. Tide Ladder — mechanism: a tall vertical scale down the left edge like a tide gauge painted on a pier, the day's hours marked up it, the water level shaded to now, each lift a horizontal tick with a vessel name, the next one bold and the bascules opening in a tiny side-view at its height; memorable: the day read as a water column; palette: river-grey water, pier-timber brown, white paint marks, one yellow buoy-mark for the next lift; type: painted gauge numerals, stencilled vessel names; sources: pier-side tide gauges (the vertical scale and painted figures), Thames tide tables (hours and heights), hospital drug charts (ticks against a time axis), pilotage buoy colours (the yellow mark)
6. Counterweight Section — mechanism: the first screen is a cutaway section through one bascule pier, the counterweight chamber drawn below the road, the deck rotated to the coming angle with the counterweight dropped to match, the next lift time written in the chamber's empty space; memorable: seeing the weight below the road that nobody knows is there; palette: section-hatched grey stone, deck steel in dark blue, void white, one orange for the moving part; type: hatched-section drawing caption, small italic engineering annotations; sources: Barry's bascule chamber sections (the cutaway), railway locomotive cutaway posters (the moving part in orange), geological strata diagrams (hatching conventions), Victorian ironwork paint schedule (the dark blue and chocolate of 1894)
7. Lift Log Ledger — mechanism: a page from the bridge-master's ledger, ruled columns for time, vessel, direction and raise, today's entries written in, the next line half-filled and underlined, a small bascule sketch in the margin at the planned angle; memorable: the next entry written in before it happens; palette: ledger cream, faded blue rules, iron-gall ink, a pencil margin sketch; type: a clerk's copperplate for entries, printed ledger column heads in a slab; sources: bridge-master's lift logs (the columns and the ruling), ship's deck log (the "direction: upriver" convention), shop ledgers with pre-ruled totals (the ruled ground), nautical chart margin sketches (the pencil bascule)
8. Ship Flag Hoist — mechanism: the vessel's name spelled in International Code flags across the top like a hoist, the next lift time beneath as the "answering pennant" line, the bascules opening to the angle drawn as two flag-like triangles that mirror the hoist; memorable: the name of the ship in flags; palette: flag primaries red, blue, yellow and white on a plain grey sky, no black; type: a signal-code Roman for the flag key, bold tabular figures for the time; sources: International Code of Signals flags (the alphabet and colours), AIS vessel feed (name, heading, destination strip), racecourse number boards (the hoist-and-change ritual), Thames sailing barge sails (the red-brown of the pennant)
9. Paint Schedule Panel — mechanism: horizontal bands in the bridge's historic paint colours, each band one lift of the day with time and vessel on it, the next lift's band the widest with the bascule angle drawn as a cut across the band; memorable: the bridge's real paint colours as the whole layout; palette: 1894 chocolate brown, 1977 red white and blue, 2010 blue and white, each a band; type: a hand-painted sign-writer's grotesque, bridge-crew stencil for the vessel name; sources: Tower Bridge paint history (the three schemes), Pantone-chip stacks (the banded swatch layout), Thames Clippers timetable boards (the time-vessel pairs), Festival of Britain signage (the sign-writer's lettering)
10. Mariner's Approach — mechanism: the screen is the river seen from a wheelhouse approaching the bridge, bascules drawn open to the angle ahead, the ship's own name and course as a HUD strip at the bottom like a chart-plotter, next lift time on the pier face where the clock tower would be; memorable: you are the ship; palette: Thames green-brown water, Portland-stone grey, dusk sky, chart-plotter amber; type: chart-plotter monospace for the data strip, carved-stone Roman for the pier time; sources: AIS chart plotters (the HUD strip and course), Thames pilotage photographs (the approach view), the bridge's stone clock faces (carved Roman time), wheelhouse brass compasses (the amber lamp tone)
11. Split-flap Lift Board — mechanism: one giant split-flap row across the top reads NEXT LIFT 14:35 and clatters over when the schedule changes, the bascules drawn beneath it as a thin elevation at their opening angle, today's other lifts as smaller flap rows stacked below; memorable: the flap clatter when the next lift rolls forward; palette: near-black flap faces, off-white letters, one signal amber for the countdown minutes, flat mid-grey ground; type: condensed grotesk in capitals for the flaps, a plain small sans for vessel names; sources: Swiss SBB departure board (the flap row and its one-number-first hierarchy), bridge control-room schedule card (vessel name, direction and gross tonnage as a stamped row), sports stadium countdown clock (minutes-only when under an hour)
12. Tide Table Page — mechanism: a newspaper-style tide-and-weather box at the top with the next lift as the largest figure, a day strip of all lifts as tick marks on a ruled horizontal line, the bascule elevation printed as a small line engraving at the opening angle; memorable: the whole day on one ruled line you can read like a tide chart; palette: newsprint white, ink black, one river blue for the lift ticks, no other colour; type: a narrow serif for the figures as in old almanacs, small-caps sans for labels; sources: broadsheet tide and weather box (the dense figured box with one big number), nautical chart (tick-mark depth scale and the engraved bridge symbol), Tower Bridge engineering drawing (the bascule elevation at angle)
13. Dot-matrix Platform Sign — mechanism: a long low amber dot-matrix strip across the top of a dark ground scrolling the vessel's name and direction, the countdown fixed on the right in big matrix digits, the bascules rendered in the same dots beneath at their open angle, later lifts as the second and third lines of the sign; memorable: everything, even the bridge, drawn in dots; palette: deep charcoal, warm amber dots, nothing else; type: dot-matrix bitmap for the sign, a quiet humanist sans in small white for the button and notes; sources: London Underground platform indicator (three-line next-train list and the scrolling line), Thames pier ferry signage (vessel and direction as one phrase), bridge lift schedule email (the exact wording of a lift notice)
14. Station Clock Face — mechanism: a large plain clock dial centred on a white ground with a single red sector swept from now to the next lift, the bascule elevation drawn as the clock's lower half at the opening angle, the vessel name under the dial, other lifts as small dots on the dial rim; memorable: the red wedge of waiting time; palette: white dial, black hands and numerals, one red for the wedge and the button; type: tall sans numerals as on a station clock, small regular sans for the rest; sources: Swiss railway clock (the dial, the red second hand and the stopped-then-jump feel), analogue kitchen timer (the swept sector for time remaining), Tower Bridge bascule chamber signage (the painted angle markings on the elevation)
15. FIDS Arrivals Wall — mechanism: a full-width columnar table on a dark blue ground, columns TIME, VESSEL, FROM, TO, STATUS, the next lift pinned at the top in a larger row with STATUS reading LIFTING IN 12 MIN and blinking, the bascules drawn in the right margin at angle; memorable: the status column flipping from SCHEDULED to LIFTING; palette: airport blue ground, white rows, a single green for on-time and amber for now; type: wide grotesk tabular figures with generous letterspacing, all capitals in the headers; sources: airport FIDS (column structure and status vocabulary), Port of London shipping movements list (FROM and TO as river reaches), shipping forecast (the clipped phrasing of the status line)
16. Field Notebook Spread — mechanism: a ruled two-page spread with the next lift written large in a hand-lettered box at the top, the bascules sketched in pencil at angle with the angle figure written beside them, today's lifts as a pencilled list with ticks for those already done; memorable: the sketch with its angle noted like a surveyor's reading; palette: pale buff paper, graphite grey, one ballpoint blue for the next lift; type: a legible monospaced face standing in for handwriting for figures, an upright sans for the button; sources: surveyor's field notebook (the ruled page and noted readings), birdwatcher's log (a list with ticks for sightings made), Tower Bridge engineers' lift log (the handwritten record of every lift and its vessel)
17. Wayfinding Totem — mechanism: a tall vertical totem on a mid-grey ground with one giant arrow pointing the vessel's direction up or down river, the next lift time and the minutes to go stacked under it, the bascule pictogram as a wayfinding icon at the open angle, later lifts as smaller arrows down the side; memorable: the arrow that tells you which way to look; palette: neutral grey ground, white, one safety yellow for the arrow; type: a heavy wayfinding sans, large and few words; sources: Legible London street totem (the vertical totem and the pictogram language), motorway sign (the arrow as the first thing read), bridge river-traffic light signals (the up or downstream distinction)
18. Concert Poster Lift — mechanism: a single bold poster-like screen, vessel name as the headline act, the next lift time as the date line, the bascules at angle as the poster's illustration, today's other lifts as support acts in a smaller list at the foot; memorable: the ship billed like a headliner; palette: flat poster red ground, black and cream ink, no gradients; type: a huge condensed woodblock-style sans for the vessel, a small sans for the times; sources: theatre and concert gig poster (headline and support hierarchy, the date line), letterpress show card (flat ink colour and tight margins), Tower Bridge lift notice (the vessel name and tonnage as billing)
19.
