# Figure and ground: the empty space is a shape too

Source: Lupton and Phillips, *Graphic Design: The New Basics*, second edition, read in the Spanish
edition *Diseño gráfico: nuevos fundamentos* (Gustavo Gili), ch. "Gestalt Principles", pp. 98-115.
Page numbers are that edition's. Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted is translated, marked and
at most one sentence.

**Principle.** In a product screen every figure separates clearly from its ground, the shapes of the
empty space are designed as deliberately as the content, and things that do the same job look alike.

## Why it holds

- p. 99: perception separates figures from ground; a black shape on black vanishes. People treat the
  ground as passive, but trained designers shape the space between and around elements as actively
  as the elements themselves.
- p. 106: figure and ground can be stable, reversible or ambiguous. Ambiguity adds energy to a logo or
  a poster; a stable relation shows a thing clearly against its setting.
- p. 102: grouping both combines and separates. Interfaces group related buttons by shared colour
  (similarity) and by position (proximity). Continuity and closure let the eye complete a line or a
  shape that is broken.
- p. 104: all typography already runs on these laws: letters group into words by proximity, weight
  and size changes signal difference by similarity, lines of text hold by continuity.
- For us: a product screen wants stable figure and ground everywhere a user acts; save ambiguity for
  the logo or one illustration.

## What to check on a screen

- Every control, card and panel has an edge or a value step that separates it from its ground.
- The gaps between blocks have even, intended shapes: no leftover slivers, no accidental holes.
- Same function, same look: all destructive actions share one treatment, all filters another.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to each capture on its own: desktop 1280x633 and phone 390x757. Invert the capture or view it
blurred, so the empty space reads as shapes.

- **PASS:** the empty space forms a few clean, deliberate shapes; every interactive figure is
  separable from its ground; elements with the same job share one look.
- **FAIL:** a figure that merges into its ground, a ragged or sliver-shaped leftover gap, or two
  elements with one job styled differently (or two jobs styled the same).

Observable: each merged figure, odd gap or mismatched pair, with where it is.
