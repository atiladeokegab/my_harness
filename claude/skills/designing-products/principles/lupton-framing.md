# Framing: the chrome recedes, margins and crops say what matters

Source: Lupton and Phillips, *Graphic Design: The New Basics*, second edition, read in the Spanish
edition *Diseño gráfico: nuevos fundamentos* (Gustavo Gili), ch. "Framing", pp. 116-127. Page numbers
are that edition's. Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted is translated, marked and at most one
sentence.

**Principle.** The interface is the frame around the user's work: it stays quiet until it is needed.
Margins, crops, captions and borders are framing choices that change how big and how important the
content feels, so make each one on purpose.

## Why it holds

- p. 117: an interface is a kind of frame; toolbars and menus sit outside the product's core job but
  are needed to use it. In the book's words, "a well-designed interface is at once visible and
  invisible: it escapes our attention when we don't need it and reappears when we do" (translated).
- p. 119: cropping redraws an image's edges and its emphasis; a selective crop can give it a new focus.
- p. 120: a wide margin presents content as an object and can make it formal; a narrow one makes it
  feel large and full to the brim. An image that bleeds off the edge looks larger and more active.
- p. 121: words frame an image's meaning; a caption or a headline over a picture changes what it says.
- p. 125: a border can merge an element with its setting or set it apart, so it adds or removes
  importance from what it holds.

## What to check on a screen

- Navigation, toolbars and panels take less visual weight than the content they serve.
- One margin system: content areas share the same outer margin at each breakpoint.
- Each crop keeps the subject (a face, the product, the data) inside the frame.
- Bleed is used only where the content should feel large; a framed box only where it should feel
  separate.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to each capture on its own: desktop 1280x633 and phone 390x757.

- **PASS:** the content area is the dominant region; chrome takes under a third of the first screen
  (our threshold); margins match; no crop cuts the subject.
- **FAIL:** chrome outweighs content, a panel or sheet covers the subject, margins differ between
  peer areas, or a crop loses what the image is of.

Observable: the share of the first screen taken by chrome, and each mismatched margin or bad crop.
