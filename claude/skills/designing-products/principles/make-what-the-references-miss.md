# Build the product you want to use and cannot find

Source: Austin Kleon, *Steal Like an Artist* (Workman, 2012), ch. 3 "Write the Book You Want to Read",
with ch. 6 "The Secret: Do Good Work and Share It with People". Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted
is marked and at most one sentence.

**Principle.** For each reference, name what it misses for our user; the product's one memorable thing
fills one of those gaps.

## Why it holds

- Ch. 3: the advice to make what you know produces dull work; make what you like and wish existed.
  Kleon extends this past books to businesses and products: build the one you want to use.
- Ch. 3: ask what your favourite makers missed, what they never made, what they could have done better
  and what they would make today. That missing piece is your opening.
- Ch. 6: when everyone is fascinated by the same thing, look at the neighbouring thing nobody is.
- For us: a product made to match its references reads as their cheaper sibling (R1) and has nothing
  of its own (R2). A product made from a named gap has its claim before it has a screen.

## What to check

- Each reference in §Intent has a line: what it gets wrong or leaves out for our user, in this scene.
- The one memorable thing names which gap it fills.
- The positioning line (`first-screen-says-what-it-is`) says the same thing in the user's words.
- No reference already does the memorable thing.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to the planning file's §Intent once references and the memorable thing are written.

- **PASS:** every reference has a gap line, and the memorable thing cites one of those gaps by
  reference name.
- **FAIL:** a reference has no gap line; the memorable thing cites no gap; or a reference capture
  already shows the memorable thing.

Observable: the gap lines and the memorable thing with its citation.

## Used at

**Intent** (step 1: the one memorable thing and the positioning). **Critique:** R2 checks the same claim
on the built screen.
