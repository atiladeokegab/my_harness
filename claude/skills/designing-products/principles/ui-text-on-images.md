# Text on an image needs steady contrast

Source: Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger, *Refactoring UI*, ch. "Working with Images": "Use good photos"
and "Text needs consistent contrast", pp. 200-206. Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted is marked
and at most one sentence.

**Principle.** Text over a photo or video must stay legible across the whole image: calm the image under
the text with an overlay, lower its contrast, tint it, or add a soft text shadow.

## Why it holds

- Photos have light and dark areas. White text that reads over the shadows vanishes over the sky.
- Ways to fix it (pp. 203-206): a semi-transparent overlay (dark for light text, light for dark text);
  lower the image's contrast and correct its brightness; colourise it with one colour (lower contrast,
  desaturate, multiply a fill); or a text shadow with a large blur and no offset, reading as a glow.
- The image itself must be good (pp. 200-201): a professional photographer or high-quality stock.
  Never design around placeholders and plan to swap in phone snaps later. Our addition: no generated
  image that looks generated.

## What to check on a screen

- Every word laid over a photo, video or illustration.
- The brightest and busiest part of the image under any text.
- Whether the imagery is real and relevant to the subject.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to each capture on its own: desktop 1280x633 and phone 390x757. On the phone the text often lands
on a different part of the image, so check both.

- **PASS:** every letter over imagery reads at a glance, including over its brightest or busiest region;
  the imagery is real and on subject.
- **FAIL:** any word partly lost against the image, text over a busy area with no overlay or tint, or a
  stock or generated image unrelated to the product's world.

Observable: the hardest-to-read word over imagery, and what it sits on.
