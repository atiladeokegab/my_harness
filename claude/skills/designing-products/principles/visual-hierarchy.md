# A clear visual hierarchy

Source: Steve Krug, *Don't Make Me Think!*, 2nd edition (New Riders, 2006), ch. 3 "Billboard Design
101", with notes from ch. 6 and ch. 9. Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted is marked and at most
one sentence.

**Principle.** How prominent a thing looks must match how much it matters; things that belong together
must look grouped, and things that are part of something must look nested inside it.

## Why it holds

- Ch. 3 gives the three traits: importance shows as prominence (size, weight, colour, space, nearness
  to the top); related things share a look or a container; parts sit visibly inside their whole, as a
  newspaper headline spans its story.
- A good hierarchy sorts the screen for the visitor before they read a word. When everything looks
  equally important they fall back on slowly hunting for words.
- A slightly wrong hierarchy (a heading spanning things that are not its children) reads like a
  garbled sentence: decodable, but it makes people stop.
- Visitors want the maker's editorial judgement on what matters most; withholding it makes them work.
- Ch. 6: the page name is usually the largest text, and left or right alignment shows structure better
  than centring. Ch. 9: when testers miss something that is there, either cut the noise or turn up the
  volume on that thing.

## What to check on a screen

- One element is clearly the most prominent, and it is the most important one.
- The steps in size, weight, contrast and spacing follow the order of importance.
- Related items share a style and a heading or container; headings span exactly their own children.
- Nothing lower in the structure sits visually above its parent.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to each capture on its own: desktop 1280x633 and phone 390x757.
1. Rank the three most prominent elements by eye (size, weight, contrast, position, space around).
2. Rank the three that matter most for the screen's job, from its heading or the brief.

- **PASS:** both rankings have the same top element; no element in the visual top three is chrome
  (logo, decoration, a secondary link, a promo).
- **FAIL:** different top elements, chrome in the visual top three, or two elements tied for loudest.

Observable: the two rankings side by side.
