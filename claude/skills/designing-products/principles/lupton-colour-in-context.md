# Colour in context: value carries the structure

Source: Lupton and Phillips, *Graphic Design: The New Basics*, second edition, read in the Spanish
edition *Diseño gráfico: nuevos fundamentos* (Gustavo Gili), ch. "Colour", pp. 80-97, drawing on
Josef Albers. Page numbers are that edition's. Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted is
translated, marked and at most one sentence.

**Principle.** Judge every colour where it sits, next to its real neighbours, and let lightness (value)
do the structural work: the screen must still read in greyscale, and text never sits on a ground of
nearly the same value.

## Why it holds

- p. 81: we see a colour relative to what surrounds it; a light tone looks lighter on dark than on
  pale. The same swatch can change once it is placed.
- p. 84: value is independent of hue and intensity. Turning a colour image to greyscale removes hue but
  keeps the tonal relations. Colours far apart in value sharpen shapes and focus attention; close
  values soften the distinctions between elements.
- p. 88: two colours of close value but different hue vibrate and look unstable; with a strong value
  difference the same colour calms down. Albers taught colour by placing it beside other colours.
- p. 90: designers pick a few colours and fix them for the project. A mid-value colour can carry both
  black and white type, and a palette with warm and cool reads as complete.
- For us: a palette approved as swatches on white can fail on the dark surface it ships on.

## What to check on a screen

- Desaturate the capture: headings, actions and states still separate from their surroundings.
- No text or icon on a ground of different hue but similar value (red on green, blue on purple).
- Each accent and state colour has been seen on every surface it appears on, in each theme.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to each capture on its own: desktop 1280x633 and phone 390x757, in colour and in greyscale.

- **PASS:** in greyscale the primary action, the active state and the main heading are still the
  three most distinct things; no vibrating hue pair carries text.
- **FAIL:** an element that stood out only by hue disappears in greyscale, or text sits on a ground of
  near-equal value.

Observable: the greyscale capture and each element that vanished, with its two colours.
