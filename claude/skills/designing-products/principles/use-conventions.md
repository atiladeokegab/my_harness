# Conventions are your friends

Source: Steve Krug, *Don't Make Me Think!*, 2nd edition (New Riders, 2006), ch. 3 "Billboard Design
101" and ch. 6 "Street signs and Breadcrumbs". Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted is marked and
at most one sentence.

**Principle.** Use the patterns people already know; invent only when the new thing needs no learning
or is worth a little, and the people you show it to are genuinely impressed.

## Why it holds

- Ch. 3: we learn the conventions of a medium without being taught (headline, caption, photo credit),
  and they let us read any paper quickly. Patterns become conventions only because they work, and they
  let people work out a screen before they read a word of it.
- Designers are tempted to reinvent because they are hired to be new and nobody wins awards for using
  conventions well. Krug's rule: innovate when you know you have a better idea, otherwise follow the
  convention, and know the value of what you replace.
- Ch. 6: navigation conventions fix a place and a look, so people find them fast and can tell them
  from content: the product's identity at the top left (for left-to-right languages) linking home,
  utilities quieter than sections, and search as a box, a button and the word Search, with no fancy
  wording, no instructions and no scope options in the box.
- Never borrow a convention's look for something that behaves differently; Krug's example is buttons
  drawn as tabs that do not act like tabs. Equally, skip a convention when its look sends the wrong
  message (ch. 7's real awards that looked like throwaway badges).

## What to check on a screen

- Product name or logo at the top left (top on phone), linking to the start.
- Navigation, account and search where the platform's users expect them; icons only in their usual sense.
- Anything that looks like a tab, button, link or input behaves like one.
- Every departure from a convention is named in the brief with its reason.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to each capture on its own: desktop 1280x633 and phone 390x757. List the standard elements
visible (identity, navigation, search, primary action, sign-in or account, back, close).

- **PASS:** each sits where the platform's users expect it (desktop: identity top left, navigation top
  or left, account top right; phone: identity top, menu or tab bar at the top or bottom edge) and looks
  like its kind, or the brief names the departure.
- **FAIL:** a standard element moved or restyled with no named reason, or one that looks like one kind
  of control and is another.

Observable: each element's found position against its expected one. The phone placements are our
adaptation; this edition predates phone layouts.
