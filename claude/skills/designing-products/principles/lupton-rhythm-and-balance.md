# Rhythm and balance: repeat a structure, then break it once

Source: Lupton and Phillips, *Graphic Design: The New Basics*, second edition, read in the Spanish
edition *Diseño gráfico: nuevos fundamentos* (Gustavo Gili), ch. "Rhythm and Balance", pp. 48-59.
Page numbers are that edition's. Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted is translated, marked and
at most one sentence.

**Principle.** A screen balances by weighing contrasting elements against each other, not by mirroring
them; a long page or a flow keeps one underlying structure and varies scale and tone along it so
something changes as the visitor moves.

## Why it holds

- p. 49: a layout feels unresolved when elements are too big, too crowded, too flat or misaligned
  against the space around them. Balance comes when visual weight is spread evenly or in proportion:
  large against small, dark against light.
- p. 50: symmetry is stable but static. Asymmetric layouts are usually livelier and still balance when
  contrasting elements counter each other.
- p. 49: most graphic design wants rhythm punctuated by change, not unbroken repetition; a book varies
  scale and tone across its pages yet keeps one structure underneath.
- p. 54: across many pages, the grid gives order to the sequence, and some surprise is what keeps the
  reader's interest.
- For us: a landing page of identical blocks is pure repetition (T4); a page where every section has a
  new layout has no rhythm at all.

## What to check on a screen

- Blurred, the screen's weight is not all on one side or all at the top unless the guide says so.
- Down a long page or along a flow: the same grid, type scale and spacing hold throughout.
- Along that sequence, at least one section changes scale or tone on purpose (a full-bleed figure, a
  dense table after open prose); no two neighbouring sections share exactly the same template.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to the full-page capture at desktop 1280 wide and phone 390 wide, and to a single-screen
capture at 1280x633 and 390x757.

- **PASS:** each single screen has no empty or overloaded quadrant you would call accidental, and the
  full page shows one structure with at least one deliberate change of scale or tone.
- **FAIL:** three or more neighbouring sections with the same template (our threshold), or every
  section built on a different structure, or a single screen with an unexplained heavy side.

Observable: the section list with each one's template, and where the deliberate change falls.
