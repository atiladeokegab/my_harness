# The first screen says what this is

Source: Steve Krug, *Don't Make Me Think!*, 2nd edition (New Riders, 2006), ch. 7 "The first step in
recovery is admitting that the Home page is beyond your control". Paraphrased in our words; anything
quoted is marked and at most one sentence.

**Principle.** Without scrolling, the first screen must answer: what is this, what is here, what can I
do, and why here rather than somewhere else.

## Why it holds

- Ch. 7: the first screen carries many jobs (identity, navigation, search, promos, sign-in), and the
  one most often lost as everyone squeezes in their item is the big picture. Insiders find it so
  obvious that they cannot see it is missing; only outsiders can.
- Krug knocks down the usual excuses: it is obvious; people find repetition annoying; the ads explain
  it; a "new here?" link will do. In tests people often say they would have used a product if only they
  had understood what it was.
- Two places carry the message. The **tagline**, visually attached to the product name, is read as a
  description of the whole product. The **welcome blurb** is a short statement above the fold.
- Use as much space as the idea needs and no more; name at most four features. A mission statement
  is not a blurb.
- A good tagline is clear and specific, about six to eight words, and says what is different and what
  the user gains. A motto states an ideal and tells a stranger nothing; cleverness is welcome only if it
  makes the benefit clearer.

## What to check on a screen

- A tagline of about six to eight words sits beside the product name and names what the product is.
- A short blurb above the fold names who it is for and what it does, with no more than four features.
- The claim is one a neighbouring product could not truthfully copy.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to each capture on its own: desktop 1280x633 and phone 390x757. Fill in "This is a ___ that lets
me ___; unlike the alternatives, it ___" using only words visible above the fold.

- **PASS:** every blank is filled from visible words (quote where each came from), and a tagline of
  roughly six to eight words sits next to the product name.
- **FAIL:** a blank needs a guess, or the only description is a slogan with no noun for what the
  product is.
