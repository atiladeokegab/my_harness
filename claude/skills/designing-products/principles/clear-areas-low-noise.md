# Clearly defined areas, and noise kept down

Source: Steve Krug, *Don't Make Me Think!*, 2nd edition (New Riders, 2006), ch. 3 "Billboard Design
101", with ch. 7 and ch. 9. Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted is marked and at most one sentence.

**Principle.** Split the screen into a few clearly bounded areas with one purpose each, and treat every
other element as noise until it earns its place.

## Why it holds

- Ch. 3: a visitor should be able to point at each area and name it ("things I can do here",
  "navigation", "today's top items"), as in the TV game where you guess a category from examples.
  Early eye-tracking work suggested people decide very fast which areas hold anything useful and then
  barely look at the others, so the areas must be distinct.
- Two kinds of noise. Busy-ness: everything shouting at once (bright colours, exclamation marks, many
  invitations to act). Background noise: lots of small bits (rules, borders, separators) that each
  seem harmless and together wear people down; Krug's fix for one menu was simply greying its lines.
- Krug's default: assume everything is visual noise until proven otherwise.
- Ch. 7: the first screen is a commons. Each added promo gives its owner all the gain and spreads the
  cost of clutter across everyone, so clutter arrives one item at a time and needs constant guarding.
- Ch. 9: when people miss something that is on the screen, cut the noise around it or turn it up.

## What to check on a screen

- Each area can be named in four words or fewer.
- Borders, rules, shadows and separators appear only where they divide areas.
- At most one element per area uses the strongest accent colour or weight; no competing calls to action.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to each capture on its own: desktop 1280x633 and phone 390x757. Mark the areas you see and name
each in four words or fewer.

- **PASS:** every visible element belongs to exactly one named area; there are at most five areas on
  desktop and three on phone (our thresholds); no area has more than one element at full accent
  colour or weight.
- **FAIL:** an element you cannot place, an area you cannot name, too many areas, or two loud elements
  in one area.

Observable: the area list, with the element that broke the rule.
