# Point, line, plane: see the screen as marks

Source: Lupton and Phillips, *Graphic Design: The New Basics*, second edition, read in the Spanish
edition *Diseño gráfico: nuevos fundamentos* (Gustavo Gili), ch. "Point, Line, Plane", pp. 32-39.
Page numbers are that edition's. Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted is translated, marked and
at most one sentence.

**Principle.** Every element on a screen reads as a point, a line or a plane. Choose which one each
element is meant to be, and let alignment draw the lines rather than rules and borders.

## Why it holds

- p. 33: point, line and plane are the building blocks; every complex layout is, at some level, their
  interaction.
- p. 34: a point marks a position. Its scale, its place and the space around it decide whether it
  stands alone or melts into the crowd. A lone status dot or badge is a point.
- p. 36: a line can be implied. When lines of text start or end at the same place, the eye joins them
  into the edge of a column, a line nobody drew.
- p. 38: a text block is a plane built of points and lines. Its density, weight or lightness comes
  from type size and the space between lines, words and letters.
- For us: a screen with many unrelated left edges has no implied lines, so it reads as scattered
  however tidy each part is.

## What to check on a screen

- The few strong vertical edges: count the distinct x positions where blocks start.
- Each text block reads as one even plane: no paragraph noticeably darker or looser than its peers
  at the same level.
- Points are deliberate: a badge, dot or icon alone in space is the thing that needs attention.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to each capture on its own: desktop 1280x633 and phone 390x757. Blur the capture (or squint)
until no word is readable.

- **PASS:** the blurred capture shows a few clear planes and points, and the main content shares no
  more than four left edges on desktop and two on phone (our threshold).
- **FAIL:** more distinct left edges than that with no grid reason, two peer text blocks of visibly
  different tone, or a lone point that is not the most important thing in its area.

Observable: the count of left edges, and any odd-toned block or stray point.

## Used at

**Look** (the specimen) and **critique**. No rubric line names it; a fail usually surfaces as B1 or
B11. `lupton-grid` sets where the edges go.
