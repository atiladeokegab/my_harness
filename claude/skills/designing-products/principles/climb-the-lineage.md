# Climb from the best reference to what it learned from

Source: Austin Kleon, *Steal Like an Artist* (Workman, 2012), ch. 1 "Steal Like an Artist". Paraphrased
in our words; anything quoted is marked and at most one sentence.

**Principle.** Pick the reference you admire most, find what it drew on, and take from that ancestor too;
the source a leader learned from is less picked over than the leader.

## Why it holds

- Ch. 1: study one maker you love deeply, then the few makers they loved, and keep climbing; that tree
  is your lineage, and your own branch starts from it.
- Ch. 1: chase every reference to its source; the most useful book is often the one the first book
  points you to.
- Ch. 1: dead and distant masters cannot refuse an apprentice; their method is left in the work.
- For us: every team in a category copies the category leader's surface, so the leader's look is
  already the median. Its ancestors (a print tradition, an older tool, a craft from the subject's
  world) give traits the neighbours do not share, which is what R2 rewards.

## What to check

- The reference list marks one reference as the best, with a one-line reason.
- For that reference, at least one ancestor is named: something its makers cite, or a clear forebear
  in design history or the subject's own craft, with a link or a citation.
- The ancestor has its own "worth taking" line, like any reference (`steal-from-many`).

## Pass or fail test

Apply to the planning file's §Intent reference list.

- **PASS:** one reference is marked best and at least one ancestor of it is listed with evidence of
  the link (a citation, an interview, a visible borrowing) and the one thing worth taking from it.
- **FAIL:** no ancestor; or an ancestor with no evidence of the link; or an ancestor that is just
  another product of the same category and era.

One hop is enough at hackathon speed (our threshold); climb further only when the first ancestor
yields nothing worth taking.

Observable: the best-reference line and the ancestor line with its evidence and its worth-taking note.

## Used at

**Intent** (step 1, choosing references). **Look:** the ancestor counts as an outside source for the
direction cards.
