# Diagram: every mark encodes something, stated once

Source: Lupton and Phillips, *Graphic Design: The New Basics*, second edition, read in the Spanish
edition *Diseño gráfico: nuevos fundamentos* (Gustavo Gili), ch. "Diagrams", pp. 214-231, drawing on
Edward Tufte. Page numbers are that edition's. Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted is translated,
marked and at most one sentence.

**Principle.** Use a diagram when the point is a relation a list cannot show; give every mark one
stated meaning (size is amount, position is time, a line is a link), choose the structure that matches
the real one, and draw no mark that means nothing.

## Why it holds

- p. 215: a diagram represents a structure, a situation or a process, and shows relations that a list
  of numbers or a verbal description would hide.
- p. 215: in a diagram, marks are coded to stand for amounts, relative size, change over time and
  structural links. Tufte argues against decorative distraction; the authors allow expressive diagrams
  as long as they stay rigorous.
- p. 216: networks differ: centralised (pyramids and trees, all from one point), decentralised (a
  spine with branches, like roads) and distributed (node to node, like the internet). The drawing
  should show which one the data is.
- p. 228: a matrix diagram puts a pair of opposing ideas on each axis. Before visual design, product
  designers map structure and user flows with Venn and matrix diagrams, decision trees and wireframes.

## What to check on a screen

- Each chart or diagram answers one stated question; its title is that answer.
- Each visual variable (size, position, colour, line) has one meaning, shown by a legend or direct
  labels.
- The layout matches the data's structure: a tree for a hierarchy, a network for peers, a timeline for
  sequence.
- No mark without data: no 3D, decorative gradient, drop shadow or icon that encodes nothing.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to each capture on its own: desktop 1280x633 and phone 390x757, on any screen with a chart,
map or diagram.

- **PASS:** you can state what each visual variable encodes from the screen alone, and the drawing's
  shape matches the data's structure.
- **FAIL:** a variable that encodes nothing or two things, a missing legend or label, or a structure
  drawn as the wrong kind (a tree shown as a flat grid of tiles).

Observable: the encoding table (variable, meaning) and each orphan mark.
