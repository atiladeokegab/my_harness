# The reservoir of goodwill

Source: Steve Krug, *Don't Make Me Think!*, 2nd edition (New Riders, 2006), ch. 10 "Usability as
common courtesy" and ch. 12 (the letter on adding "sizzle"). Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted
is marked and at most one sentence.

**Principle.** Every visitor arrives with a limited store of goodwill; each obstacle drains it, and
candour and saved effort refill it.

## Why it holds

- Ch. 10 opens with an airline whose site said nothing about a strike on the very day every ticket
  holder wanted to know about it. Clarity is not enough: the product must also behave decently.
- The store differs from person to person and from moment to moment, it can be refilled, and a single
  blow (a registration form with dozens of fields) can empty it.
- What drains it: hiding what people want (support contact, delivery costs, prices); making them
  reformat their input; asking for data the task does not need; fake sincerity; "sizzle" in the way
  (long intros, heavy decorative images); a sloppy, amateur look.
- What refills it: making the top few tasks obvious and easy; stating awkward facts up front (costs,
  fees, outages); saving steps; visible effort; real, current answers to real questions; a graceful way
  back from errors; an apology when you cannot do what they want.
- Ch. 12: most people come to get something done, not to be engaged. Sizzle belongs on entertainment,
  pure-brand and portfolio sites. A user-hostile choice may still be a fair business call, but make it
  knowingly.

## What to check on a screen

- Cost, limits, time and data use are visible where the decision is made.
- No splash, intro animation or promotional overlay stands between the visitor and the content.
- Error and empty states say what happened and offer the way forward.
- The top three tasks are reachable from the first screen.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to each capture on its own: desktop 1280x633 and phone 390x757. For the screen's main decision
or task, list what a cautious visitor needs before committing: cost, time, what happens to their data,
how to get help, how to undo.

- **PASS:** each item that applies is visible or one plainly labelled click away, and nothing (splash,
  intro, promotional overlay) covers the content.
- **FAIL:** name the hidden item or the blocking element.

Observable: the list of needs, each marked shown, one click away, or hidden.
