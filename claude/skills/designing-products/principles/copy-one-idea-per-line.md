# One idea per line, the point first

Sources: Digital.gov, "Plain language guide series: Clear and short" (adapted from PlainLanguage.gov),
digital.gov/guides/plain-language; GOV.UK, "Writing guidelines: Use clear language" and "Create a clear
structure for your content", guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk. Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted
is marked and at most one sentence.

**Principle.** Each sentence, bullet and card carries one idea, and its first words carry the point.

## Why it holds

- Digital.gov: "Express only one idea in each sentence." It keeps paragraphs to 150 words, in three to
  eight sentences.
- GOV.UK asks writers to split sentences over 25 words, keep paragraphs to five sentences, and put the
  most important information first. It notes that people read only 20 to 28% of the text on a page and
  skim the rest, so the first words of each line are often the only ones read (`design-for-scanning`).
- Two ideas in one line force a choice the reader did not ask for: which of the two is the point? A
  product screen has less room than a guidance page, so the rule is tighter here.

## What to check on a screen

- No running sentence over 25 words (GOV.UK's line); on product screens, our threshold is 20.
- No sentence or bullet joins two claims with "and", a semicolon or a dash.
- Cards and bullets in a set each make one claim, in parallel form.
- Read only the first three words of each line: the point is still there.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to each capture on its own: desktop 1280x633 and phone 390x757. Read every sentence and bullet of
running text. The phone capture wraps the same words into more lines; judge the sentence, not the wrap.

- **PASS:** every sentence is 20 words or fewer, carries one idea, and leads with its point.
- **FAIL:** quote each failing sentence with its word count and which rule it breaks (length, two ideas,
  point buried).

Observable: the quoted sentences with counts, or "none".

## Used at

**Critique** (the copy pass in the finish check), and the **UX** step when the first screen's lines are
drafted.
