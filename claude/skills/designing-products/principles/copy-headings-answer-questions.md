# Headings answer the visitor's question

Sources: GOV.UK, "Writing guidelines: Create a clear structure for your content" and "A to Z style guide"
(FAQs), guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk. Paraphrased in our words; anything quoted is marked and at
most one sentence.

**Principle.** Each heading answers a question the visitor has at that point, stated as the answer with
its key words first. Never the question itself, never a slogan.

## Why it holds

- GOV.UK wants headings descriptive, front-loaded and active, and rejects question headings: "they're hard
  to frontload and users want answers, not questions". GOV.UK does not use FAQs at all.
- A scanning visitor reads the headings and little else (`design-for-scanning`), so the headings alone
  must tell the story: what this is, whether it fits me, how it works, what it costs, how to start.
- A slogan heading ("Built different", "The future of audit") answers nothing, and a bare label
  ("Features", "Why us") only names a box. Both make the visitor read the body to find the point.
- Our adaptation: on a first screen, the visitor's questions come from the surface brief's audience and
  job, in order; each section's heading answers the next one.

## What to check on a screen

- Write down the visitor's questions for the page in order (from the surface brief). Each heading answers
  one, as a statement.
- Read only the headings, top to bottom: they make a sensible summary of the page.
- The first two or three words of each heading carry its point; no heading ends in a question mark.
- Navigation labels may stay short nouns ("Pricing"); page and section headings may not.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to each capture on its own: desktop 1280x633 and phone 390x757. Copy out every heading in view.

- **PASS:** a stranger given only the headings can say what the product does and what to do next, and no
  heading is a question, a slogan or a bare label.
- **FAIL:** quote each failing heading, its kind (question, slogan, label, buried point) and the visitor
  question it should answer.

Observable: the heading list with each failure marked, or "none".

## Used at

**UX** (the information hierarchy and the first screen's sections) and **critique**.
