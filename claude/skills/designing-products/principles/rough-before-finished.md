# Generate in a rough medium, finish in the final one

Source: Austin Kleon, *Steal Like an Artist* (Workman, 2012), ch. 4 "Use Your Hands". Paraphrased in our
words; anything quoted is marked and at most one sentence.

**Principle.** Produce many cheap, rough options before any is made in the finishing medium, and when a
finished piece stalls, go back to the rough medium rather than tweaking.

## Why it holds

- Ch. 4: the computer is good for editing ideas and readying them for release, and poor at producing
  them; it invites editing before there is anything to edit.
- Ch. 4: Kleon cites a cartoonist who keeps away from the computer until most of the thinking is
  done, because once work enters the finishing tool it starts sliding towards done.
- Ch. 4: Kleon sequenced a whole book by printing each piece small and shuffling the sheets by hand;
  laying the parts out together shows an order that a list hides. His loop is rough, then finished,
  then rough again when energy drops.
- For us: an agent's finishing medium is styled code. The first styled specimen anchors every later
  one, and tuning its CSS feels like progress while the direction stays unchosen.

## What to check

- §UI holds a rough round first: one-line directions (a mechanism, a scene, a palette in words), no
  styled code.
- The specimens are picked from that round by a blind cut on rough sketches; only the eight that
  survive are finished (`reference/tournament.md`).
- When a specimen is reworked and still fails the judge, the next move is a return to the rough list,
  not another CSS pass.
- Sequence the tour and the first-screen story from a contact sheet of all the captures side by side,
  not from the list of steps.

## Pass or fail test

Apply to the planning file's §UI.

- **PASS:** 24 or more rough one-line directions (our threshold; the tournament's round 0 asks for 30)
  appear before any specimen, and each specimen names the rough line it grew from.
- **FAIL:** the first artefact in §UI is a styled specimen, or fewer than 24 rough lines precede
  the specimens.

Observable: the count of rough lines, and each card's link back to one.

## Used at

**Look** (step 3, the tournament's rounds 0 and 1, before any specimen is rendered). **UX:** the tour's step order is settled on a
contact sheet.
